use the Same key for both encryption and decryption. ^6iZdgfxI

Unlike symmetric systems, Public-Key Cryptography (PKC) uses two different keys: ^2mDElPf6

1. **Public Key ($pk$):** Known by **everyone**. Used to **encrypt** messages or verify signatures. ^SNpDf5bY

2. **Private Key ($sk$):** Known only to the **owner**. Used to **decrypt** messages or create signatures. ^B1nUBmp4

1. **The Mailbox Analogy:** Anyone can put a letter in the mailbox (using the Public Key), but only the owner with the key can open it (Private Key). ^34huRZeM

2. **Feasibility:** The keys are mathematically related, but it is computationally infeasible to derive the Private Key from the Public Key. ^zMvSHefP

3. **History:** Invented by Diffie and Hellman (1976) to solve [[key distribution problems]] and enable digital signatures. ^t3PAbPXt

Formal Definition : A PKC system consists of three algorithms. ^YluGsgMu

- $E(pk, m)$: Encrypts message $m$ into ciphertext $c$. ^cpNuTDaw

- $D(sk, c)$: Decrypts ciphertext $c$ back to message $m$. ^tXoQlL84

To understand RSA, you must understand modular arithmetic. ^3Yjq6aMG

- $a \equiv b \pmod n$ means $a$ and $b$ have the same remainder when divided by $n$. ^EPm7bvp3

- **Property:** $((a \pmod n) + (b \pmod n)) \pmod n = (a + b) \pmod n$. (This applies to multiplication as well). ^XynHf9rA

- **Additive Inverse:** A number added to $x$ to get $0 \pmod n$. ^GM7qLUos

- **Multiplicative Inverse ($x^{-1}$):** A number multiplied by $x$ to get $1 \pmod n$. ^SH59QJgY

- _Note:_ The multiplicative inverse only exists if $x$ and $n$ are **relatively prime** (their Greatest Common Divisor, GCD, is 1). ^meM93o6d

- **$\phi(n)$ counts how many positive integers less than $n$ are relatively prime to $n$. ^7DxoyyAi

* ***Crucial for RSA:** If $p$ and $q$ are prime numbers, then  [[] $\phi(pq) = (p-1)(q-1)$ ]]. ^NiFHsbTs

Invented by Rivest, Shamir, and Adleman (MIT, 1977). Its security relies on the difficulty of [[factoring]] large integers. ^7XK87MU9

1. Choose two large prime numbers $p$ and$ $q$. ^OAIcGPgJ

2. Compute $n = p \times q$.(This $n$ is the [[modulus]]) ^EhVi95Ge

3. Compute Euler's function $\phi(n) = (p-1)(q-1)$ ^B8WrzK09

4. Choose a public exponent $e$ such that 1 < $e$ < $\phi(n)$ and $gcd(e, \phi(n)) = 1$. ^3mAuoOMl

5. Calculate the private exponent $d$ such that $e \cdot d \equiv 1 \pmod{\phi(n)}$. (This means $d$ is the multiplicative inverse of $e$) ^GjtE3jEV

- Calculate $d=7$ (because $3 \times 7 = 21 \equiv 1 \pmod{20}$). ^90yQCG2y

- $M = 17^7 \pmod{33} = 8$. (Original message recovered). ^WAY0U9PX

- **Scenario:** Encrypting Message "HI" where $M = 89$. ^PFp4OFwW

- **Result:** The decrypted message is 23, NOT 89. ^npQ4kDjk

- **Why? (Slide Question):** RSA requires that the message $M$ must be smaller than the modulus $n$ ($M < n$). Here, $89 > 33$, so [[the information was lost during the modulo operation before encryption even finished]]. To fix this, you must use a larger $n$ or split the message into smaller blocks. ^9pdIgsNn
